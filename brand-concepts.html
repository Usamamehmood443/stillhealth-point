The brief we're designing to

The mark should sit quietly inside the feeling

“When someone spends a few minutes on the website, I'd like them to come away feeling that they've found someone who understands the complexity of their health concerns and can help them make sense of what they're experiencing. To me, that feeling is ultimately more important than any individual logo, graphic, or design element.” Michael, 20 June
That line shaped every decision here. None of these three announce themselves. They are quiet, geometric and timeless rather than expressive, the kind of mark that still looks right in ten years and never competes with the words on the page.
Direction One

The Stillpoint

01

The Stillpoint

Closest to the writing already on the site

Rings radiating out from a still centre, the calm point from which a whole system can finally be seen clearly. It's the idea the homepage already opens with, given a form.

The important detail: the rings are deliberately imperfect. They sit at uneven intervals and each one opens a little wider than the last. Perfectly concentric circles read as a target or a wifi symbol; this asymmetry is what makes it read instead as something settling, a system finding its centre rather than a bullseye.

Direction Two

Equilibrium

02

Equilibrium

The quietest of the three

A circle meeting its own horizon, a form held in balance, exactly level. The line runs past the circle on both sides, so the shape sits within something larger than itself rather than floating free.

Why it might be right: it is almost nothing. No decoration, no gesture, nothing that dates. It reads as balance and as a horizon at the same time, and it's the least likely of the three to ever feel like a trend you have to move on from.

Direction Three

Within

03

Within

The most direct answer to your philosophy

A person held inside their environment. The outer ring is open rather than closed, an environment that acts on you rather than a container that seals you in.

The deliberate choice: the inner form sits off-centre. A person isn't at the tidy middle of their circumstances; they're somewhere inside them, adapting. That small asymmetry is what stops this being a generic circle-in-a-circle, and it's the part that speaks most directly to how you describe your work.

How each one holds up as it shrinks

A logo spends most of its life small: a browser tab, a search result, a profile picture. This is the honest test, and it is where the first direction needed work: at 16px the full four-ring version turns to mush. Below 24px all three switch to a simplified variant with fewer elements, heavier strokes and the same recognisable shape. That is standard practice, and it is shown here rather than hidden.

The recurring element

Beyond the logo itself

You asked whether Stillpoint could have one subtle visual element that becomes recognisable throughout the site. That turned out to be the more demanding half of the request, and it changed how these were judged. A mark that cannot produce a working motif is a weaker direction, so each one is tested against that here rather than assumed.

01 The Stillpoint

This makes a genuinely beautiful quiet background element. Set very large and cropped by the corner of a page, or sitting faintly behind a section, it works better than either of the other two.

It does not, however, produce a heading rule or a bullet. Nested arcs cannot become a rule without inventing a curve so slight it disappears in print, and a single arc fragment at bullet size is hard to read. We could have filled the gap with something plausible, but you asked for one element that carries across the whole site, and this direction only delivers part of that.
02 Equilibrium

The strongest motif of the three, and nothing had to be invented to find it. The mark already is a rule with a circle on it.

A rule under a heading is the most common element in design, so the rule itself is not ownable. What is ownable is that every rule in this system extends past its container into the margin on both sides, which is the mark's overhang promoted into a layout rule. That is distinctive, and it holds consistently across a whole site.

If you want our view

We would choose Within

You asked for several concepts before settling on a direction, so all three are here as real options. But you have told us a great deal about how you think, and it would be a waste not to give you a recommendation you can push back on.

Your own description of your work is about the relationship between a person and their environment, and about symptoms as meaningful responses to that environment rather than isolated faults. Within is close to a direct statement of that: something held inside a form that is open, that acts on it, that does not seal it in. The gap is the whole argument of the mark, and it is your argument rather than a borrowed one.
What we give up by choosing it. Equilibrium has the stronger motif and behaves better at very small sizes. Those are real advantages and we do not want to pretend otherwise. If the recurring element across the site matters more to you than the mark itself, Equilibrium is the better answer and we would be comfortable building it.
Where Within is weak. An open ring sits near loading-spinner territory, and its bullet is the least distinctive element in the set. Neither is fatal, and both are manageable in how the mark is used.
The wordmark

Set in the same voice as the site

The name is set in Fraunces, already the typeface of every heading on the site, so the logo and the writing speak as one thing rather than two. The tagline sits beneath the name in Inter, at a size that lets it be read without competing.

In the finished files the lettering is converted to outlines rather than live text. That means the logo renders identically for a printer, a sign maker, or anyone opening the file on a machine that does not have Fraunces installed. It is a small detail that prevents a common and expensive kind of mistake.

Colour

The palette, made official

These colours were chosen while building the site rather than as a deliberate brand decision you were ever asked to approve. This is the moment to confirm or change them, and it is far cheaper to change them now than later. Two values were quietly failing legibility standards and have been corrected.

What was corrected. The small uppercase labels that appear on every section of the site, “MY APPROACH” and “PATIENT EXPERIENCES”, sat at 3.1:1 contrast, below the accessibility standard for text that size. The warm accent was at 2.75:1. Both now pass comfortably. Given how many of your patients live with brain fog, light sensitivity and visual strain, this felt like the wrong place to let a design preference win.

Over to you

Three questions

A rough reaction is genuinely more useful than a considered one. We'd rather know early that something isn't you.

Does one of the three resonate, or is one clearly not you?

You don't need to choose a winner. Knowing which to stop working on is just as useful. Whichever direction you lean toward, we'll refine it properly and come back with it applied across the site.

Should the tagline live inside the logo, or only on the site?

You liked the idea of carrying "Where complexity becomes clarity" through the branding, so it sits in the lockups above. The alternative is to keep the logo to the name alone and let the tagline do its work in the writing. Both are defensible, and it is genuinely a preference. The third lockup shows the same question for your name, which is worth answering at the same time.

Is there any colour here you don't like?

The greens and warm neutrals came out of building the site, not out of a brand decision you were ever asked to approve. If they aren't right, now is a far cheaper moment to change them than later.

One thing worth raising. “Still point” is also a specific term in craniosacral therapy, and a number of other practices use the Stillpoint name. Neither is a problem and neither is a reason to change anything, but it is worth knowing whether you would like the mark to lean gently toward that association or deliberately away from it.
